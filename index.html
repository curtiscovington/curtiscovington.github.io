THE PERSON BEHIND THE NOTES

About Me

I’m a software engineer, independent AI researcher, and someone who has never been particularly good at leaving an interesting question alone.

My fascination with artificial intelligence started long before the current wave of large language models. In high school, I discovered ELIZA, an early conversational program whose simple responses managed to make a computer feel surprisingly alive. Not long afterward, I built a neural network to play tic-tac-toe. It wasn’t particularly sophisticated, but the underlying question stuck with me: How much of what we call intelligence can emerge from relatively simple machinery?

I’ve spent more than a decade building software, working across different industries and systems. Today, I’m a Senior Core Infrastructure Engineer at Oracle, working on the distributed systems behind Oracle Cloud Infrastructure’s Compute Control Plane. I also hold a Master’s degree in Computer Science from the University of Colorado Boulder.

Outside my professional work, I pursue independent AI research, primarily focused on mechanistic interpretability and autonomous agents. I run experiments on open-weight language models using local hardware, including an NVIDIA DGX Spark, investigating questions like:

  • Can we identify meaningful concepts inside neural networks and causally influence their behavior?
  • How do models represent, retrieve, and manipulate information internally?
  • Why do AI agents sometimes possess the information necessary to solve a problem but fail to use it?
  • What happens when agents can share knowledge, collaborate, and pursue goals over extended periods?

My experiments have explored sparse autoencoders, activation steering, Jacobian lenses, agent memory, and more. Some have produced encouraging results. Others have fallen apart under stronger controls. I find both outcomes valuable. I publish my methods, measurements, code, and failures because I believe good research should be reproducible, not just impressive.

My interests aren’t limited to AI. I founded an educational technology company that worked with Waco Independent School District, experimented with game development, and built countless tools and prototypes simply because an idea caught my attention. I’m drawn to philosophy, science fiction, the nature of consciousness, and the strange relationship between technological progress and the human experience.

I tend to approach the world with a mixture of curiosity, skepticism, and optimism. I think we’re living through an extraordinary period in human history, and that artificial intelligence may fundamentally reshape what civilization is capable of. I’m excited by that possibility, but I also think extraordinary claims deserve extraordinary scrutiny.

Ultimately, I don’t want to be someone who merely watches the future arrive. I want to participate in building it, understanding it, and occasionally discovering something nobody thought to look for.

This website is where I document that process.
